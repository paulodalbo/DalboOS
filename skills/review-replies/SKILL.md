# Review replies and Google Business Profile posts

Replies are read by the reviewer once and by every future customer who checks the listing. They should sound like the owner wrote them on a good day: specific, short and human.

Work from the active client's `memory/voice.md` and `memory/business.md`. If no client is active, ask which one.

## Getting the reviews

Ask the user to paste the reviews with the star rating and the reviewer's name as shown, or read them from a connected Google Business Profile tool if the session has one. Do not invent or paraphrase a review to reply to.

## Writing a reply

- **Use something from the review.** Name the job, the staff member or the detail they mentioned. A reply that could sit under any review is worse than none.
- **Two to four sentences.** Five-star reviews with no text get one warm line.
- **The client's voice and spelling**, signed the way the client signs (first name, or the business name).
- **Vary them.** A page of identical openings looks automated. Do not start every reply with thanks.
- **No keyword stuffing.** The suburb and service belong in a reply only where a person would naturally say them.

### Critical reviews

1. Acknowledge what went wrong for them, in their terms, without arguing the facts in public.
2. Apologise if the business was at fault. If the user says it was not, say you are sorry they had that experience without admitting something untrue.
3. Offer one direct way to sort it out: a name and a phone number or email.
4. Stop. No excuses, no explaining policy at length, no asking them to change the rating.

Before writing, ask the user what actually happened if the review makes a factual claim. The reply must not contradict what the business knows to be true, and must not reveal anything private about the customer: no details of their job, health, payment or address beyond what they posted themselves.

### Reviews that look fake or abusive

Say so to the user and explain the options: flag the review to Google through the Business Profile, and post a brief, calm reply for other readers ("We can't find a record of this visit. Please contact us on ... so we can look into it."). Do not accuse the reviewer of lying.

## What this skill will not do

- Write reviews, or replies posing as customers. Fake reviews break Google's rules and consumer law in Australia and the US.
- Offer discounts or gifts in exchange for a review or for changing one.

If the client wants more reviews, write a short, honest request they can send to real customers after a job, with their review link.

## Google Business Profile posts

When asked for posts or updates:

- One idea per post: an offer, a finished job, a seasonal reminder, an event.
- Lead with the point in the first sentence; keep it under about 80 words even though the limit is higher.
- A real photo from the client beats a generated one for a local listing. Ask for one.
- End with the action button to use (Call, Book, Learn more) and the link.
- Offers need the dates and conditions the client confirmed.

## Output

Save replies to the client's `content/<YYYY-MM>/review-replies.md`, each under the reviewer's name and rating, ready to paste. List any review where you need the user to confirm facts before the reply is used.
