# Local SEO for a service-area or shopfront business

For a local business, most search demand ends in the map results and the business profile, not in ten blue links. This skill checks the things that decide that, in order of effect, and ends with a short list of actions the agency can deliver.

It is deliberately narrow. For a full technical site audit or a content strategy for a national brand, use a dedicated SEO skill pack alongside this one.

Work from the active client's `memory/business.md` and `memory/focus.md`.

## 1. What to check, and how

Use web search and page fetching where the session has them. Where it does not, ask the user for screenshots or pasted text. Record what you saw and when; mark anything you could not check as "not checked", never as fine.

**Google Business Profile**
- Primary category matches the main money-making service; secondary categories cover the rest.
- Name is the real business name, with no added keywords.
- Address or service areas, hours (including holidays), phone and website link are right and match the website.
- Services and products are listed with descriptions.
- Photos are recent and real. Posts in the last month.
- Reviews: count, average, how recent, and whether they get replies.

**Website**
- Each main service has its own page that says what it is, where it is offered and how to book.
- Title tags and headings name the service and the area in natural words.
- Name, address and phone on the site match the profile exactly.
- Click-to-call and a contact option are reachable on a phone without scrolling far.
- Pages load quickly on mobile and are readable without zooming.
- `LocalBusiness` structured data (or a more specific type) with name, address, phone, hours and area served.

**Area pages**
- A page for a suburb or city is worth having when the business really serves it and the page says something specific to it: jobs done there, travel time, local conditions. Thin pages that swap only the suburb name do more harm than good, so recommend few and real over many and templated.

**Citations**
- The business details are consistent on the main directories for the country. In Australia that typically includes Yellow Pages, True Local and industry directories; in the US, Yelp, BBB and industry directories. Also Apple Maps and Bing Places in both.

**Competitors**
- Search the main service plus the suburb and note who holds the top three map results, and what they have that the client lacks: review count, categories, photos, pages.

**AI assistants**
- Ask an AI assistant with web access the question a customer would ("who's a good emergency plumber in Southport?") and note whether the client appears and what is said. Assistants lean on the same public sources: the business profile, reviews, directories and clear service pages. The fixes are the ones above, plus an FAQ on the site that answers real customer questions in plain sentences. There is no reliable trick beyond that; do not promise placement in AI answers.

## 2. Prioritise

Sort findings by effect and effort. Fixes to the profile's category, details and reviews come first because they are fast and matter most; new pages and citations follow.

## 3. Write it up

Save as `reports/local-seo-<YYYY-MM>.md` in the client's folder:

1. **Where they stand**: three or four sentences, with who outranks them and the clearest reason.
2. **Do first** (this week): up to five actions, each with what to change and why.
3. **Do next** (this month).
4. **Ongoing**: the review request routine, monthly posts, photos.
5. **Not checked**: anything you could not verify.

Rankings are not guaranteed by anyone. State what each action is expected to improve, not a position.

## 4. Offer the follow-through

Offer to draft what the list calls for: service page copy, the profile's service descriptions, the structured data block, the review request message (see the `review-replies` skill).
